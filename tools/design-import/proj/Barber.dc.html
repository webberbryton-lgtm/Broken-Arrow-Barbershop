02 / In the chair

Sit down. He’ll take it from there.

He looks at your head shape, your growth patterns, your hairline and how your hair behaves, then tells you what the cut should be and why. Clients book him because they want that call made by someone who knows. You don’t need to bring a plan.

  1. 01

03 / Scissor cuts

Scissor work influenced by European barbering.

Bryton does fades and clipper work too. That came naturally to him, and he saw a gap in his scissor work, so he went looking for training. He has since received training from barbers all over the world, including barbers from Europe, through mentorship and classes, and specializes in scissor haircuts: cuts done mostly or entirely with shears instead of clippers. Scissors leave the hair softer and give longer styles more movement as they grow out.

04 / Hair systems

Every system in the shop goes through him.

A hair system is a thin base with real hair that’s attached to your scalp and cut into your own hair. Bryton color-matches it, fits it and cuts it in so it blends. Maintenance visits are with him too, so the same person looks after it every time.

05 / Teaching

He’s taught other barbers how to cut.

Bryton is a licensed barber instructor. He taught hundreds of students at Paul Mitchell The School Provo, and still teaches a class from time to time, showing new barbers how to read a head shape, blend a fade and talk a client through a change. The same habits go into every cut he does at the shop.
